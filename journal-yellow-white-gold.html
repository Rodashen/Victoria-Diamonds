White gold vs yellow gold: colour, purity and care

By Victoria Diamonds · 18 October 2026 · 3 min read · English · Updated 24 October 2026

What is the difference between white gold and yellow gold?

Colour comes from the alloy and any surface finish, while the karat mark describes gold purity. White and yellow gold marked 18K both contain 75% gold. Some white gold has a rhodium finish that may need maintenance. Choose for appearance and the care the actual piece needs; colour alone does not establish quality.

Yellow and white gold offer different colours, but colour is not the same as purity. Two pieces marked 18K contain the same proportion of gold even if their alloy mixtures and surface finishes look different.

What gives gold its colour?

Pure gold is yellow. Jewellery alloys combine it with other metals to change properties such as colour and working characteristics. Yellow-gold recipes often include silver and copper. White-gold alloys may include metals such as palladium or nickel; exact recipes vary.

An 18K alloy contains 75% gold whether it is yellow or white. The remaining alloy ingredients and manufacturing process matter when comparing performance. Ask about the actual alloy if you have a known metal sensitivity.

Neither colour belongs to one skin tone or diamond colour grade. Compare the complete setting in daylight and indoor light. The surrounding metal can affect your impression of a diamond, so use the stone’s documentation as well as its appearance.

Understand rhodium plating

Some white-gold jewellery has a thin rhodium finish that gives a bright white appearance. Where used, that layer can wear with friction and expose the underlying alloy colour. Replating frequency depends on the piece and how it is worn; there is no universal schedule.

White gold and platinum are different materials, not interchangeable names. GIA’s guide to white jewellery metals explains these differences. Ask a jeweller what finish is present before choosing a maintenance treatment.

Choose care for the setting as well as the metal

Clean gently and avoid harsh chemicals. Do not assume ultrasonic cleaning is suitable simply because the metal is gold: stones, treatments, settings and the condition of the piece also matter. Professional polishing removes some material, so it should be considered rather than routine excessive treatment.
