9K, 14K, 18K or 22K gold: how to choose

By Victoria Diamonds · 18 October 2026 · 3 min read · English

In this articleRead karat and fineness correctlyWhat changes between the purities?Do not choose from a hardness ranking aloneCompare complete quotationsChoosing gold in the Victoria Diamonds customiser

Gold karat describes gold content, not a simple quality or durability ranking. Choose a purity alongside the design, alloy, finish and intended wear. A higher number means more gold; it does not automatically mean a better choice for every piece.

Read karat and fineness correctly

Karat expresses gold content in parts out of 24. Millesimal fineness expresses the proportion in parts per thousand. It is separate from gemstone carat, which measures weight.

Common UK gold fineness marks and their meaning
Gold descriptionFineness markGold content represented
9K / 9 carat37537.5%
14K / 14 carat58558.5% for the 585 standard
18K / 18 carat75075%
22K / 22 carat916Approximately 91.6%; 22/24 is about 91.67%

The fraction 14/24 is about 58.33%, but the UK 585 standard represents 58.5%. Avoid treating those figures as exactly the same. A full hallmark provides more information than a fineness number alone; consult the hallmarking standards published by the London Assay Office.

What changes between the purities?

9K contains a larger proportion of other metals and is often a more accessible price option. 14K offers more gold content while leaving scope for different alloy properties. 18K contains three-quarters gold and is widely used for fine jewellery. 22K has a high gold content and is often associated with a richer yellow appearance.

Colour is not a reliable test of purity. Alloy recipe and surface finish can change what you see; yellow and white alloys can have the same karat rating. The cover image is an editorial illustration, not a calibrated colour comparison.

Do not choose from a hardness ranking alone

Pure gold is soft and malleable. Alloying and working processes can change hardness and strength, but karat alone cannot predict the exact durability of a finished ring or clasp. Thickness, setting construction, heat treatment and wear all matter.

A substantial, well-made piece and a very fine setting may perform differently even at the same purity. Ask whether the proposed alloy and design suit the intended stones and routine. We have omitted generic Vickers-hardness numbers because an unspecified alloy cannot support a dependable comparison.

Compare complete quotations

Gold content influences material cost, but a finished price also includes metal weight, workmanship, stones and construction. Two pieces at the same karat are not necessarily equivalent in cost or design. Ask what is included before comparing totals.

Choose the colour you enjoy and a design appropriate for the way you will wear it. Read yellow versus white gold for finish and maintenance considerations, and our care guide for cleaning advice.
